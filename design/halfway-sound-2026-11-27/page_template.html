Wharf WOD, the halfway cue

Is the halfway sound the right one?

The shape is right. The level and the pitch are the weak points. It is the quietest cue in the app and it plays on the change beep's exact note. Four alternatives are below, each inside a real Tabata round, with the one I would build marked.

What the sound is today

Shape
Right

Two quick pips. Every other beep in the app sounds once and long, so a double pip cannot be read as a count or a change when it is heard clearly. I checked whether the 60 ms gap blurs into one sound in an echoing room, over 200 simulated rooms: it holds.

Level
Quietest cue in the app

2.5 dB (decibels) under the change beep, and 200 ms of sound against 770 ms. That is about a tenth of the change beep's energy. It was made gentle on purpose on 14 November. With music on and the phone on the floor it is the cue most likely to be missed.

Pitch
Same note as the change beep

1031 Hz (hertz), identical to the beep that ends every round. Only the rhythm separates "halfway" from "round over". The file is called mid, but nothing about its pitch is in the middle.

Each strip shows pitch up the side and time along the bottom, drawn to scale. The dashed outlines are the change beep and the 3, 2, 1 beep at their real length. A thinner bar is a quieter sound.

How these were made, and what is not proven

The clips are the app's own cue log. The timer's real cue code wrote out what it plays and when, and each clip mixes the shipped beep and voice files at those times. Only the halfway file is swapped.

The new sounds are synthesised. Each uses the harmonic recipe measured in the shipped beeps, so it sounds like the same family. With a voice on, the loudest pairing peaks at 0.4 dB under full scale, the same headroom as today's loudest change beep with a line on it.

The noise is a stand-in. It is set so the change beep sits 6 dB over the noise in its own band. It shows which sounds survive a masker, not how loud your gym is.

No standard exists to copy. Timers that have a halfway alert treat it as an optional extra beep; I found no shared convention for what it should sound like.
